reported, on that day in some time zone. Nothing from an earlier day is
presented as 19 November news; where a story's underlying event fell on

## What was excluded, and why

- **Stories dated 18 November or earlier** (for example the GTA VI review
  embargo lifting and the Metacritic 99 first appearing on 18 November, the
  Fed minutes' underlying meeting of 27–28 October, the NSA outage first
  reported on Tuesday): used only as context for 19 November developments,
  never presented as 19 November events themselves. The GTA VI item is the
  launch-day rollout, which is a 19 November event.
- **Unverifiable or single-aggregator claims** with no named outlet behind
  them (for example some items in AI-generated daily digests): dropped
  rather than risked.
- **Forecasts and speculation shown as fact**: the Shenzhen meeting is
  described as a meeting with no deal expected (per analysts), not as an
  agreement; Anthropic's $1.5tn valuation is described as an estimate; the
  trilateral's agenda is described as reported, not as outcomes.
- **Rumours**: the Nvidia CEO-departure speculation around a "sex scandal"
  in some aggregators was left out; only the verified facts (resignation,
  acting CEO, pay announcement) are used.
- **Local-only items** (Greek parliamentary timetables, school-assembly
  lists, individual court cases): real but too small for a one-page world
  summary; breadth across regions and fields took priority.

## How the image was made

`draw_frontpage.py` (PIL, Noto Serif/Sans) renders the page from the story
list in this file's companion script: masthead, lead story with a flat
illustration drawn from basic shapes (globe, skyline, three banners — no
copied artwork, no photos), a four-column grid grouped by section, a UTC
timeline strip, and a date-check footer naming the sources. `main.py`
copies the rendered `entry.png` to the output directory and verifies it.
